# CapCar partnerships, events and editorial drafts

Research checked **22 October 2026**. Frankfurt am Main is the founder's chosen local area. All proposals, invitations and pitches are drafts. No partnership, permission, speaking slot, attendance or coverage is implied. These options serve the same conversion as the [growth plan](growth-plan.md): add a real car and save one useful planned job.

## Five realistic no-cost partner approaches

Send at most **one** partner proposal during the month, after review. Partners are a reserve channel; do not contact all five to fill the list. If the same person is in the creator CSV, use one relationship and one conversation. No shared mailing lists, account access, customer imports, free professional services, discount codes, exclusivity or promised promotion.

The reusable asset is the original job card in [community-opportunities.md](community-opportunities.md). An expert can review the fields; they do not certify CapCar or the owner's vehicle. Each party approves any use of its name/logo or quotations before publication. A simple written agreement should settle the asset, review effort, optional distribution, attribution and which aggregate outcomes can be shared. Each keeps its own contacts.

## Online session: “Bring one job from your car's list”

**Purpose:** help up to three willing owners turn one real pending job into a saved plan, and learn where the process is confusing. Capacity is a proposed host limit, not a scarcity claim. This is an organizational exercise, not mechanical instruction.

**Proposed date:** Wednesday **11 November 2026, 19:00–19:20 CET**, subject to participant agreement. This is a newly proposed CapCar session, not an existing event being advertised. Use an existing free call account/tool that the founder already knows. No new account, paid webinar platform, signup page, automatic email sequence or recording is needed.

**Format:** 0–3 minutes demonstrate a paper job card; 3–7 show a labelled public example; 7–15 participants privately enter their car/next job or complete the paper card; 15–20 discuss the one confusing step. Participants control their own accounts, sign-in and screens. Do not ask them to reveal receipts, plates, VINs or credentials. Use paper if someone would prefer not to create an account. No one must allow analytics to attend.

**Preparation checklist, spread across days 11, 15, 18 and 19 of the growth plan:**

- Recheck the actual saving path on the founder's existing account; label public example data.
- Prepare the original job card and one brief illustration. Test screen sharing; keep personal tabs closed.
- Invite only people who expressed interest, or place a public invite where explicitly allowed. Confirm the timezone and send the join link privately to those who request it.
- Confirm the call tool's existing free plan can handle 20 minutes. If it cannot, use another already-available tool or cancel; do not buy one.
- Ask attendees not to share private vehicle data in the call. Recording is off. Take only minimal, paraphrased workflow notes with permission.
- If fewer than two people want the session, offer the same 10-minute help to an interested person within an existing creator-help block instead. Do not manufacture attendance.

Invitation draft:

Bring the job, a part reference if you have one and an estimate if you know it. We'll make a useful job card first, then I can show how to keep it with your car in CapCar. I build the product. Follow-up, only when requested or to a participant who agreed to receive it:

If you want to keep your plan in CapCar, start at capcar.dev and save it with your car after signing in. Was there one step that stopped the record being useful? A short answer is enough. **Day-of budget:** setup 5 + session 20 + requested follow-up 10 + replies/log 10 = **45 minutes**. Target hypothesis: two participants save a useful plan or complete the paper card. ## Three credible editorial angles

These are service/story hypotheses, not established news trends. None depends on invented users, partners, traction or savings. The founder being solo comes from the task context; biography, personal car ownership, funding and launch dates remain unverified and should not be supplied to an editor without confirmation.

### A1. A project estimate and a repair bill mean different things

Show a clear job-card method that distinguishes project budget, planned work and actual spending. CapCar's live BMW example separates a €300 budget, €30 estimate and €28 actual example cost; those figures are **illustrative**, not a customer's result, quote or measured saving. [Live example](https://capcar.dev/), [implementation notes](../garage-onboarding-and-build-story.md). Editorial payoff: readers can use the method in a notebook immediately. Evidence packet: public example capture with its label, original job card and a short factual explanation of one job moving from planned to complete.

### A2. Owner-recorded history needs its context

A useful build record can retain the work/date/cost while staying distinct from professional inspection. Optional Passport sharing and private-by-default vehicle records are explicit product boundaries. [Platform](https://capcar.dev/platform), [record/sharing documentation](../garage-onboarding-and-build-story.md). Editorial payoff: a practical discussion of what a record actually tells the next reader. Avoid resale-value, inspection validity or legal-evidence claims. No claim of customer data loss or a widespread industry problem without evidence.

### A3. A solo founder sets limits on automotive previews

The founder can discuss building a beta around a useful next task while keeping exact-car visualization, native OBD scanning and recording predictions clearly bounded. [Studio](https://capcar.dev/studio), [Sound Studio](https://capcar.dev/sound-studio), [availability](https://capcar.dev/platform). Editorial payoff: demonstrate what a reference model or sound clip can and cannot tell an owner. The founding motivation and personal garage story must come from the founder, not this draft. This angle is weaker until the founder provides a real personal decision to discuss.

## Concise pitch email: GRM first

Paste as a message only after the founder checks the contact route and actual demo. Approximately 120 words; substitute a real signature.

```text
Subject: Keeping estimates out of a project’s actual costs

Hello GRM editorial team,

I have a small workshop-organization example for your DIY readers: keep a project's budget, planned parts costs and actual spending separate.

I build CapCar, a free-beta garage for car plans and records. Its public BMW example follows one cabin-filter job from a plan to an owner-recorded completion. The amounts are labelled illustrative, and the method works on paper too.

The useful detail is recording the final job total once when it already includes a purchased part. That keeps a parts list from quietly becoming a misleading spend total.

Would this suit a short practical piece or independent product look? The example is at https://capcar.dev. I can answer questions or supply a clearly labelled capture if useful.

[Founder’s real name]
CapCar · capcar.dev
```

No invented founder quote or promised material is attached. Capture assets before offering them in a stronger pitch. Keep a local draft note of the date, route and angle. One initial pitch is enough for this campaign; a follow-up would need genuinely new, relevant information and must replace another activity block. No coverage is an acceptable outcome.
