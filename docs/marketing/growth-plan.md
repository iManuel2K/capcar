# CapCar: one car, one useful next step

Research checked **22 October 2026**, Europe/Berlin. Draft campaign: **23 October–21 November 2026**. Move the relative day numbers if starting later. All material is for founder review. Research and drafting are complete; nothing has been sent or published.

## Decision

Focus on **DIY owners with an unfinished road-car project and a specific next job**. First-time builders and restorers fit when they have that same immediate need. Driving, sound and concept content can introduce CapCar, but the campaign asks everyone to follow one path:

**Useful contribution or post → capcar.dev → Create your garage → car → goal → first planned job → account/sign-in → explicitly save → open the saved plan.**

Promise: **“Keep your next job, parts plan and costs with the right car.”**

Ready-to-copy description:

> CapCar is a private digital garage for your car's plans and records. Add your car and save the next job you want to tackle. You can keep planned costs separate from actual spending as the work progresses. Browse first; an account is needed to save. It's free during beta: capcar.dev.

This is a **public beta activation campaign**. Public browsing and self-serve account entry already exist; beta wording is live. Evidence does not establish product-market fit, retained usage, reliable live account delivery or a completed production acceptance gate. “Early access” is a useful internal stage analogy, not an invented invite restriction. The strongest honest story is the small step between a forgotten idea and an owner-maintained vehicle record. It is premature to pitch a traction milestone or broad industry trend. [Live homepage](https://capcar.dev/), [platform availability](https://capcar.dev/platform), [beta release criteria](../../BETA-LAUNCH.md).

## First-week experiment

Question: **Will an owner who sees a concrete planning method save a real next task?** Hypothesis: a specific “save your next job” invitation will produce more useful actions per founder minute than a general feature introduction. This is a small qualitative pilot, not a statistically powered A/B test.

Use the same public homepage and the same first-plan CTA. Compare two discovery routes without changing application code:

- **Community route:** one allowed/disclosed feedback contribution after moderator approval. If approval is absent, keep participating and use only consented inbound conversations; do not force a post to fill the experiment.
- **Creator route:** two personally researched, deduplicated invitations, prioritizing 2 Skint 2 Scrap and Retro Rebuild. Ask whether one current task would fit. Provide the link only as a natural next step or in the approved message route.

Record minutes, qualified conversations, consented visits, registration submissions, confirmed accounts where aggregate owner access permits, cars added and first useful actions. Use `community_pilot` and `creator_pilot` UTM content tags, not creator names. Keep routing identical; the comparison is directional because audience, timing and permissions differ.

**Learning thresholds, not forecasts:** seek five useful conversations and three first useful actions in week one. For the month, use 30 qualified visits, 10 confirmed signups, eight car additions and six first useful actions as initial planning hypotheses. They are not promised outcomes or industry benchmarks. The small sample may produce none. At day seven, continue the route with the clearest task-saving evidence per hour, rather than the highest views. If fewer than five conversations occur, report insufficient evidence and run another week at the same volume. If people reach setup but cannot save, stop invitations and document the exact blocker for a separate product task.
